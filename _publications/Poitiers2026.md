Large eco-evolutionary studies require realistic and dynamic environmental frameworks, used both as a spatial background and as stress variables. This seminar presents a multi-layered reconstruction of the environmental evolution of continental Africa over the past 30 million years, integrating mantle-driven dynamic topography, crustal tectonics, plate motions, and volcanic dynamics into a continuous elevation model, subsequently used as a boundary condition for climatic and physiographic simulations.
